Mark Your Confidence

Before revealing each answer, write C if you are confident or G if you are guessing. Then check.

This produces four categories, and only one of them is dangerous. Confident-and-right means you know it. Guess-and-wrong means you know you do not. Guess-and-right means you got lucky and should study it. Confident-and-wrong is the killer — you believe something false and have no reason to look it up. That category is invisible on an ordinary score report, and it is what fails people on the first attempt.

📝 Unit 1 Practice Set

Answer, mark C or G, then reveal.

Question 1 · Obj 3.5

A technician needs a motherboard for a compact chassis that supports exactly one expansion card. Which form factor best fits?

Reveal Answer
Mini-ITX. At 6.7 by 6.7 inches it carries a single expansion slot. microATX supports up to four and ATX up to seven — both are larger than the requirement.
Question 2 · Obj 3.5

What is the maximum amount of RAM a 32-bit operating system can address, and why?

Reveal Answer
Approximately 4 GB. A 32-bit address space allows 232 unique addresses, which equals about 4 billion bytes. Exceeding that ceiling requires a 64-bit OS.
Question 3 · Obj 5.1

A user reports their desktop loses the correct date and time every time it is unplugged overnight. What is the most likely cause?

Reveal Answer
A failing CMOS battery — typically a CR2032 coin cell. It maintains firmware settings and the real-time clock when the system has no power.
Question 4 · Obj 3.3

Which memory type detects and corrects single-bit errors, and where is it typically deployed?

Reveal Answer
ECC (Error Correcting Code) memory. It is standard in servers, where a silent bit flip could corrupt data unnoticed. It requires motherboard and CPU support. Parity memory detects errors but cannot correct them.
Question 5 · Obj 3.5

A graphics card is installed in a slot that is physically x16, but performance is below expectations. What should the technician check?

Reveal Answer
Whether the slot is electrically wired for fewer lanes than its physical size suggests. A physically x16 slot may be wired x4. The motherboard documentation specifies this; the slot's appearance does not.
Question 6 · Obj 3.6

What are the three standard DC voltage rails supplied by an ATX power supply?

Reveal Answer
+3.3 V, +5 V, and +12 V. The +12 V rail carries the heaviest load, feeding the processor and graphics card.
Question 7 · Obj 5.1

A system powers on, fans spin, but there is no video output and the system emits a repeating beep pattern. What is happening, and what is the first thing to check?

Reveal Answer
The POST has detected a fault and is reporting via beep codes because video is not yet available. Reseat the memory modules first — RAM is the most common cause — then the video card. Beep code meanings are defined by the firmware manufacturer.
Question 8 · Obj 5.1

Why should a technician never wear an anti-static wrist strap while working on a system connected to mains power?

Reveal Answer
The strap creates a low-resistance path to ground through the technician's body. Its purpose is protecting components from static, not protecting people from electricity. Always disconnect mains power before service.
Question 9 · Obj 3.5

What is the difference between a TPM and an HSM?

Reveal Answer
A TPM is a chip or firmware feature on an individual system that generates and stores keys tied to that machine — BitLocker relies on it. An HSM is a dedicated appliance or card providing key generation and storage at scale, typically for servers and certificate authorities.
Question 10 · Obj 5.1

A user reports their computer runs fine at startup but becomes noticeably sluggish after about twenty minutes of heavy use. What should be investigated first?

Reveal Answer
Thermal throttling. The processor is reducing its own speed to protect itself from overheating. Check for dust-clogged heat sinks, failed or reversed fans, and degraded thermal paste.
Question 11 · Obj 3.3

What must be true for two memory modules to operate in dual-channel mode?

Reveal Answer
They must be matched in capacity and specification, and installed in the specific slot pair the motherboard designates for dual-channel — usually indicated by matching slot colors. The manual is authoritative.
Question 12 · Obj 5.1

Place these troubleshooting steps in the correct order: verify full system functionality; test the theory; identify the problem; document findings; establish a plan of action; establish a theory of probable cause.

Reveal Answer
1. Identify the problem. 2. Establish a theory of probable cause. 3. Test the theory. 4. Establish a plan of action. 5. Verify full system functionality. 6. Document findings, actions, and outcomes.
Question 13 · Obj 3.6

A technician is asked to service a power supply that has stopped working. What is the correct action?

Reveal Answer
Replace the unit. A PSU is never opened — its capacitors retain a lethal charge after disconnection, and there are no user-serviceable parts inside.
Question 14 · Obj 3.6

What does an 80 PLUS Gold rating indicate about a power supply?

Reveal Answer
A certified efficiency level — how much of the input power reaches components rather than being lost as waste heat. Higher tiers run cooler and cost less to operate. It says nothing about total wattage capacity.
Question 15 · Obj 5.1

A newly built system will not power on at all — no fans, no lights — but the power supply tests good and mains power is confirmed. What is a likely cause?

Reveal Answer
The front panel header is connected incorrectly or not at all. The power switch wires to individual header pins on the motherboard, and a misconnection produces a system that appears completely dead.

Acronym Drill

15 minutes · Do it in short bursts, not one sitting

CompTIA publishes the acronym list the exam draws from, at the back of the official objectives PDF. Beginners drown in initialisms, and the fix is spaced repetition rather than cramming.

This week, drill these: ATX · BIOS · CMOS · CPU · DDR · DIMM · ECC · EPS · ESD · HSM · ITX · PCIe · POST · PSU · RAM · SO-DIMM · TPM · UEFI · VRM

Three sessions of five minutes beats one session of fifteen. That is not a productivity slogan; it is how memory consolidation works.
