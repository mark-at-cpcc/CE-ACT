Think Before You Click

Before selecting an answer, decide honestly whether you actually know it or you're guessing. That private judgment matters more than the score. Confident-and-right means you know it. Guessed-and-wrong means you know you don't — an easy fix. Guessed-and-right means you got lucky and should still go read the section. Confident-and-wrong is the one to watch for: you believe something false and have no reason to double-check it. That category is invisible on an ordinary score report, and it is what fails people on the first attempt.

Select an answer for each question, then click Grade My Answers to see your results. Click Reset for a brand-new random set of 10, and keep going. You are expected to repeat this until you score a perfect 10 out of 10 in a single attempt. Anything less than 10, reset and try again — a fresh random set every time means you cannot simply memorize positions, you actually have to know the material.
