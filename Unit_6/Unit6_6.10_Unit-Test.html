The final unit test for the course, drawing from all five exam domains.

Technical Overview

The Unit 6 test is cumulative. Items are drawn from every domain on the 220-1201 blueprint and weighted to match the published domain percentages rather than the content of any single unit. Take it in Brightspace using the link for this section.

Test Format

ItemDetail
CoverageAll five domains: mobile devices, networking, hardware, virtualization and cloud, and hardware and network troubleshooting
WeightingMatched to the published 220-1201 domain percentages
Item typesMultiple choice, including scenario-based items that describe a symptom and ask for a diagnosis or next step
AttemptsUnlimited before the deadline. Items are randomized, so no two attempts are identical.
FeedbackReleased after submission so you can review what you missed
MaterialsClosed book. Treat every attempt as if it were the real exam.
Unlimited Attempts Are Not a Shortcut

Retakes exist so you can close gaps, not so you can grind until the randomizer hands you a favorable set. A student who retakes with the notes open is training a skill the testing center will not let them use. Take each attempt cold, review what you missed, go back to the material, and then take it again.

Test Taking Skills

These apply as much to the live CompTIA exam as to this test. The final week is the right time to make them habits.

Read the Last Sentence First

Scenario items bury the actual question at the end after a paragraph of setup. Read the final sentence, learn what is being asked, then go back through the scenario knowing what you are looking for. Half the detail in a long stem is deliberately irrelevant, and you can only tell which half once you know the question.

Eliminate Before You Select

Find the wrong answers first. Most items include at least one option that is technically real but does not fit the scenario, and one that would be correct for a closely related question. Cutting to two before choosing turns a guess into a decision, and it is faster than evaluating four options on their merits.

Answer the Question That Was Asked

“What should the technician do next” and “what is the cause” have different correct answers for the same scenario. Watch for next, first, most likely, best, and least. Those words are doing real work, and missing one is the most common way a prepared candidate loses points.

Flag It and Move

You have roughly one minute per item on the live exam. If an item is not resolving in ninety seconds, pick your best option, flag it, and move on. Three easy items at the end of the exam are worth more than one hard item in the middle, and a surprising number of flagged items resolve themselves once a later question reminds you of something.

Trust the First Read on Recall, Not on Reasoning

If an item tests a fact you either know or do not, your first instinct is usually right and second-guessing costs you. If an item requires reasoning through a scenario, re-checking is worth the time. Knowing which kind of item you are looking at is the skill.

Submit Your Proof

Complete the test through the Brightspace link for this section by the due date. After your final attempt, review the feedback and note every item you missed, along with every item you got right but were unsure about. Bring that list to your remaining study time.

Before You Schedule the Real Thing

This test measures what you know. It does not, on its own, tell you that you are ready to sit for the 220-1201. That call comes from your performance on full-length practice exams under exam conditions, and your instructor will review that with you before a voucher recommendation is issued. If your results here are uneven across domains, say so — it is far cheaper to have that conversation now than after a failed attempt.
