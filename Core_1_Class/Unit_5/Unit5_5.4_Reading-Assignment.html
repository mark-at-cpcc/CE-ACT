Why This Unit's Reading Is Lighter

Every other unit runs 10 hours or more of reading. This one runs closer to 8, and that is deliberate: Unit 5 carries three NETLAB labs and your first full-length timed practice exam. The hours you save here are spent in the pod and on the exam — the total week still lands at 19 to 21 hours. Use the freed time on the practice exam rather than treating this as a light week.

Do Chapter 6's Performance-Based Question

Chapter 6 closes with one — work it this unit. Chapter 10's performance-based question sits at the end of the chapter, in the pages you read back in Unit 1.

Objectives 2.2, 2.10, 2.11, 4.2, and 4.3 are all "Given a scenario" objectives — five of this unit's seven. That is the highest proportion of any unit in the course, so expect performance-based items and scenario questions rather than definitions.

Note-Taking That Earns Its Time

  • Threat and control, side by side. Two columns: the Unit 4 attack, and the Unit 5 control that stops it. This single sheet is the most useful page of notes in the course.
  • Draw the permissions problem. Sketch a share with NTFS on one side and share permissions on the other, and work three examples until predicting effective access is automatic.
  • One line per setting: where it lives and what it prevents. Especially for the browser settings in objective 2.11, which are numerous and easily confused.
  • Backup types as a table. What each copies, how long it takes, and what a restore requires. Then answer one question in writing: full on Sunday plus incrementals each weekday, and a failure Thursday afternoon — what do you need?
  • Write the question, not the answer. "Which encryption protects a USB drive?" on one side, BitLocker To Go on the other.
Read Chapter 6 With Something in Front of You

Much of Chapter 6 describes settings you can open right now — Windows security settings and every browser setting in objective 2.11. Read those sections with the setting on screen. The router and Active Directory material is the exception; you get hands-on time for that in NETLAB Labs 19 and 10.

What Good Notes Look Like

Reading notes are not graded, and they are still the highest-value hours of your week. Aim for your threat-and-control sheet, worked permissions examples, one line per browser setting, a backup type table, and your Review Question results with guessed answers marked — in your own words rather than transcription. Bring them to class; we use them. See the Course Schedule for the target date.
