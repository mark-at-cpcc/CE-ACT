## Part 1 — The Target

CompTIA A+ Core 1 (220-1201): maximum 90 questions, 90 minutes, multiple-choice plus performance-based, scored 100–900 with **675 to pass**. CompTIA's stated candidate profile assumes roughly 12 months of hands-on IT support experience.

| Domain | Weight |
|---|---|
| 1.0 Mobile Devices | 13% |
| 2.0 Networking | 23% |
| 3.0 Hardware | 25% |
| 4.0 Virtualization and Cloud Computing | 11% |
| 5.0 Hardware and Network Troubleshooting | 28% |

**Plain-spoken:** Hardware plus hardware troubleshooting is 53% of this exam. Networking is another quarter. Students need their hands on equipment in week one, and troubleshooting has to be a daily habit rather than a unit we save for the end.

**The honest constraint, on the record.** We are compressing a certification written for someone with a year of field experience into six weeks with beginners. That is achievable, but only with a mastery gate before students schedule the exam. A student who finishes at 62% on practice exams and tests anyway will fail, and that failure will read as a course failure. The gate is in Part 6.

### The Weekly Rhythm — Kolb Mapped to Meeting Days

| Day | Kolb Stage | What Happens | Bloom |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tuesday (3 hr)** | Concrete Experience → Reflective Observation | Open with the thing itself. Hands on hardware, a dead machine, a tangle of cable. Students touch it before they have vocabulary for it, then debrief what they noticed. | Remember → Understand |
| **Wednesday (3 hr)** | Abstract Conceptualization | Now we name it. Direct instruction, demonstration, terminology, objective framing, the why. | Understand → Apply |
| **Thursday (3 hr)** | Active Experimentation | Structured lab plus a troubleshooting ticket. Students do it, break it, fix it, document it. Exit assessment. | Apply → Analyze |

Adults need to know *why* before they invest in *what*. Putting the concrete experience on Tuesday creates the felt need that makes Wednesday's terminology stick. Beginners who get the lecture first have nothing to hang it on.

## Part 3 — Pacing and Load

**Instructor ruling:** reading budgeted at 10+ hours per week.

| Component | Hrs/Week | 6-Week Total |
|---|---|---|
| Sybex Volume 1 reading | 10.0 – 11.0 | 60 – 66 |
| Professor Messer video + structured notes | 2.0 – 2.5 | 12 – 15 |
| Independent hands-on / take-home lab | 2.0 – 2.5 | 12 – 15 |
| Practice questions, flashcards, acronym drill | 2.0 – 2.5 | 12 – 15 |
| Reflection journal + discussion | 1.0 | 6 |
| **Out-of-class total** | **17.0 – 19.5** | **102 – 117** |
| **Including contact hours** | **26.0 – 28.5** | **156 – 171** |

**What this ruling buys us.** At 60–66 hours against a Core 1 volume of this size, students read at a genuinely careful annotate-as-you-go pace rather than a skim. Two consequences, both good:

1. The tiered "Core Read / Reference Skim" structure from Revision 1 is **dropped**. Everything is required reading.
2. There is budget for a **structured second pass** in Weeks 5–6 over Chapters 1, 2, 12, and 13 — the highest-weighted material. A second exposure to Hardware and Troubleshooting immediately before the exam is worth more than any amount of new content.

**What it costs.** Roughly 26–28 hours per week including class is a near-full-time commitment. This number goes in the syllabus and the Week 0 announcement in plain language, so students self-select honestly on day one rather than discovering it in Week 3.

**Placement logic.** Labs were mapped to the unit whose hardware or concepts they exercise: storage and printer labs with Unit 2's storage and output content, command-line and network-settings labs with Unit 3's networking foundations, sharing and remote-access labs with Unit 4's network services, and virtualization with Unit 5. Unit 6 carries Labs 12, 16, and 17 per instructor direction — installing software, Windows troubleshooting tools, and security and privacy make a coherent integration set for the final week.

**[NEEDS YOUR RULING] — Core 2 content in a Core 1 course.** A substantial portion of the NDG A+ v5 series targets **Core 2 (220-1202)** objectives rather than Core 1: Windows management and customization, Control Panel, users and groups, sharing resources, installing software, security and privacy, and scripting are all Core 2 domains. Only Labs 01A/01B, 06, 07, 13, 14, 18, and 19 map cleanly onto Core 1.

Three options:
- **Assign all 21 as designed.** Students get broader exposure and a head start on Core 2, at the cost of hours spent on material this exam does not test.
- **Assign only the Core 1–aligned labs** (8 labs, ~6–10 hours total). Tighter alignment to the first-attempt-pass goal; the rest are held for a Core 2 course.
- **Assign Core 1 labs as required and Core 2 labs as optional enrichment.** Keeps the exposure available without charging it against a 17–19 hour week.

**[NEEDS YOUR RULING] — Workload impact.** NETLAB adds **2.25 to 6.25 hours per week** on top of the existing 17–19 hour out-of-class budget. At the heaviest weeks (Units 4 and 5, five labs each), total out-of-class time reaches **21–26 hours**, and total weekly commitment including class reaches **30–35 hours**. That exceeds the pacing target set earlier in this plan. Either the reading budget comes down, the lab distribution flattens, or the target moves.

**Platform conventions** (carried from existing CPCC course practice): launch labs from Brightspace rather than the NETLAB site directly; reservations required; sessions must complete in one sitting or reset; pods are finite so early-week booking is essential; screenshots pasted into the lab quiz question field, never attached as files, with NETLAB timestamp and reservation visible; unlimited reattempts before the deadline, each requiring a new reservation; lab tools used only inside NETLAB, never on college or personal networks.

### Bloom Progression

| Week | Level | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Remember → Understand | Identify components on sight; explain function |

## Part 4 — Textbook Mapping

**Text:** *CompTIA A+ Complete Certification Kit: Core 1 Exam 220-1201 and Core 2 Exam 220-1202* (Sybex, 6th ed.), ISBN-13 978-1394350056 — Docter & Buhagiar.

**Scope:** Volume 1, Chapters 1–13. Volume 2 (Chapters 14–23) is Core 2 and out of scope.

**Two assets in the front matter worth naming.** The book includes an **Assessment Test for Exam 220-1201** with answer key — that becomes the Week 1 diagnostic, written to the exam rather than authored by us. And every chapter carries at least one Performance-Based Question, with Chapter 1 carrying two: **14 PBQs total**. PBQs are the scarce resource in A+ prep and the thing most first-attempt failures have never practiced. This is the bank.

**Two design decisions worth defending in review:**

**Chapter 3 splits.** Peripherals go to Unit 2, cables and connectors to Unit 3 — matching exactly how Messer's 3.2 videos were already split.

**Chapters 12 and 13 are dismantled and distributed.** The book concentrates all troubleshooting at the end. We spread it across five units so each diagnostic skill lands the same week students learn the underlying component. Domain 5 is 28% of the exam, and a novice cannot diagnose a drive failure the same week they first learn what a drive is — but they can two days after installing one.

**Chapter-level assignments, per instructor direction.** Assignments name chapters and section headings rather than page ranges. Actionable for students; the one thing it costs is the ability to verify weekly reading hours are balanced. Unit 4 currently looks heaviest and Unit 3 lightest. Flagged in QA.

## Part 5 — Visual Design Specification

### Palette

Sampled from the CPCC logo and consistent with the CISSP CE welcome announcement and the ACT A+/Net+ Pre-Course Success Kit.

| Token | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `--cpcc-gold` | `#B8962E` | Rules, borders, section dividers, callout accents, large headers, links |
| `--cpcc-gold-light` | `#F5EECF` | Callout and highlight backgrounds |
| `--cpcc-gold-mid` | `#D4B455` | Secondary accents, hover states |
| `--cpcc-black` | `#000000` | All body text and headings |
| `--cpcc-gray-dark` | `#4A4A4A` | Header/footer bands, table headers |
| `--cpcc-gray-mid` | `#5A5A5A` | Captions, metadata, secondary labels |
| `--cpcc-gray-light` | `#F4F4F2` | Section backgrounds, table striping |
| `--cpcc-border` | `#E0D5B0` | Container borders |
| `--white` | `#FFFFFF` | Page background |

**Contrast verification.** True black `#000000` on white is 21:1 — the maximum achievable, passes WCAG AAA with the widest possible margin. Gold `#B8962E` on white is approximately 3.3:1 — passes AA for large text and UI components, **fails for body text**. Gold is therefore restricted to rules, borders, dividers, and headings at 18pt+/14pt bold or larger. This is a hard rule in the build.

**Deviation from prior builds, intentional.** The CISSP CE announcement and the ACT Pre-Course Success Kit both used charcoal `#4A4A4A` for type. This course uses true black by instructor direction. Charcoal is retained for header bands and table headers, where it separates structural chrome from content. The practical effect: maximum legibility for a beginner audience reading 10+ hours a week, including on low-quality displays and in printed handouts.

### Structure

The component architecture from the existing course template is retained — objectives box, key concept, definition box, callouts, lab section, activity section, progress bar, navigation, summary box. Only the `:root` variables change. Additional adjustments:

- **Emoji removed** from CSS `::before` content on callouts and key-concept boxes. Screen readers announce them as content. Replaced with gold geometric rules and text labels.
- **Web-safe font fallback stack.** Brightspace's HTML editor frequently strips Google Fonts `<link>` tags. Modules must degrade to a clean system sans-serif, not Times New Roman.
- **Body typeface:** Georgia serif for prose, matching the CISSP and ACT builds. Sans-serif for headings, tables, and labels.

### Weekly Announcements

Eight total: Week 0 welcome, Units 1–6, and a send-off. Posted the Monday before each week opens. Fixed four-beat structure:

1. **Where we are** — one warm sentence of orientation
2. **What you'll be able to do by Friday** — objectives in plain language, not CompTIA-speak
3. **Your week at a glance** — reading, video, lab, due dates in a scannable block
4. **One thing that trips people up** — a specific, concrete heads-up

Encouraging and direct, no cheerleading. Beginners in an accelerated cert course are already anxious. What steadies them is knowing exactly what's coming and hearing that the hard part is normal.

### Grading Model — Completion Coursework + Scaled Final

**Instructor direction:** every assignment carries a 100% grading percentage; the final is scored consistent with the A+ exam.

**Category A — Coursework (completion).** Every graded item outside the final is worth 100 points and awarded in full when it meets the completion standard.

| Item | Count | Points Each |
|---|---|---|
| Course Orientation Quiz | 1 | 100 |
| Unit Pre-Assessments | 6 | 100 |
| Lab & Hands-On Submissions | 24 | 100 |
| NETLAB Online Labs | 21 | 100 |
| Practice Question Sets | 6 | 100 |
| Reflection Journals | 6 | 100 |
| Unit Tests | 6 | 100 |

**Completion standard, stated explicitly.** Full credit requires meeting a defined standard, not merely uploading a file. Each item's standard is published on its Brightspace page — a lab requires all required screenshots plus a documented outcome; a reflection requires responding to all prompts; a unit test requires completion with review of missed items. Work that falls short is returned for revision and resubmitted for full credit rather than partially scored. This preserves your 100% structure while keeping rigor, and it is the single most important guardrail here: a 100% category with no standard behind it teaches students that submission equals mastery, which is exactly the belief that fails people at the testing center.

**Category B — Final Comprehensive Exam (A+ scaled).**

### Final Exam Scoring — Modeled on 220-1201

**Verified exam facts.** Maximum 90 questions. 90 minutes. Multiple-choice plus performance-based. Scored on a **100–900 scale with 675 to pass**.

**Item distribution, weighted to the published domain percentages:**

| Domain | Weight | Items |
|---|---|---|
| 1.0 Mobile Devices | 13% | 12 |
| 2.0 Networking | 23% | 21 |
| 3.0 Hardware | 25% | 22 |
| 4.0 Virtualization and Cloud | 11% | 10 |
| 5.0 Hardware and Network Troubleshooting | 28% | 25 |
| **Total** | **100%** | **90** |

**Point structure.** 85 multiple-choice items at 1 point each, plus 5 performance-based items at 3 points each with partial credit awarded — **100 raw points total**. Weighting PBQs at 3× approximates CompTIA's practice of scoring performance items more heavily than single multiple-choice items. No penalty for wrong answers, matching the real exam.

**Raw-to-scaled conversion.** The A+ scale runs 100–900, so the floor is 100 rather than zero:

> **Scaled Score = 100 + (Raw Points × 8)**

| Raw | Scaled | Result |
|---|---|---|
| 100 | 900 | Perfect |
| 90 | 820 | Strong pass |
| 85 | 780 | **Mastery gate threshold** |
| 80 | 740 | Comfortable pass |
| 75 | 700 | Pass |
| **72** | **676** | **Minimum pass** |
| 70 | 660 | Fail — close |
| 65 | 620 | Fail |
| 60 | 580 | Fail |
| 50 | 500 | Fail |

**Honest limitation, and it matters.** CompTIA does not publish its scaling algorithm. The 675 cut score is **not** 75% — because the scale begins at 100, it corresponds to roughly 72% of raw points under a linear model. The real exam also uses item-level psychometric weighting and includes an undisclosed number of unscored pretest items, neither of which can be reproduced. **This model is a close approximation, not a replica.** It is the closest defensible mapping available from published information, and its practical purpose is teaching students what 675 actually feels like, so nobody walks in believing a comfortable 70% is a pass.

**Reporting.** Brightspace displays the raw percentage for gradebook purposes; the student-facing score report leads with the **scaled score out of 900** and a pass/fail against 675, followed by a per-domain breakdown showing exactly where points were lost.

**The mastery gate.** Voucher recommendation issued after **two consecutive full-length practice exams at 85 raw / 780 scaled or above**. The gap between 780 and the 675 cut is deliberate margin — it absorbs test-day nerves, unfamiliar item phrasing, and the harder PBQs on the live exam. This is the single most important element for the stated course goal, and I'd hold it firm.

## Part 7 — Design Elements Awaiting Approval

**1. See It Before You Name It.** Unit 1 opens with hands on real hardware — students identify components before any vocabulary is introduced, so the terminology has somewhere to attach. This is a Week 1 device only; later units do not carry a continuing hardware build.

**2. Ticket of the Day.** Every meeting opens with a realistic help-desk ticket in the customer's own words ("it's making a clicking sound and now my files are gone"). Fifteen minutes of triage using CompTIA's methodology. This is how Domain 5 earns its 28% without a dedicated week.

**3. Explain It to the Customer.** Sixty-second drill explaining a concept to a non-technical listener. Hits Understand and Evaluate simultaneously, and it's the actual job.

**4. Confidence-Weighted Practice Questions.** Students mark each answer confident or guessing. Confident-and-wrong is the category that fails people, and it's invisible on a plain score report.

**5. Acronym Drill.** Fifteen minutes of spaced drill per session against CompTIA's published acronym list. Low cost, disproportionate return for beginners drowning in initialisms.

**6. Domain Heat-Map Dashboard.** Self-contained HTML tracker showing coverage and confidence across all Core 1 objectives. *Highest build effort of the six — easiest to cut.*

### Sequencing Alternatives

- **Option A (designed above)** — Foundation → Network → Mobile/Cloud → Integration. Best on-ramp for beginners; troubleshooting distributed. Cost: objective mapping zigzags, so the crosswalk works harder.
- **Option B** — Domain-sequential (1→2→3→4→5). Cleanest mapping. Cost: opens with Mobile Devices, abstract for someone who has never opened a computer, and quarantines 28% of the exam into the final week.
- **Option C** — Troubleshooting-first spiral. Motivating for experienced learners, wrong for this population. You cannot diagnose a component you cannot name.
