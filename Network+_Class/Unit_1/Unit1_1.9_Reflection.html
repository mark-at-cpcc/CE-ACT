Why This Exists

Experience alone does not produce learning. A lab, a NETLAB session, or a ticket teaches you comparatively little unless you stop and process what happened — what surprised you, what you predicted wrongly, what you would do differently. That step is the hinge of real learning from experience, and it is the one adults skip most often because it feels less productive than doing. It is not.

The Prompts

Respond to every prompt, directly in the fields below. Write in prose, in your own voice — this is not a formal paper and it should not read like one.

What a Strong Response Looks Like
“I was sure the cable with the loose boot was the bad one, and the tester passed it; the bad one was the clean-looking Cat 6 with pins 3 and 6 open. I had been diagnosing by appearance, which is a Layer 1 problem I did not know I had.” That is specific, honest, and connected. It is also about a mistake — most of the best ones are.
Completion Standard (100 points)

Every prompt answered in connected prose, about 100 words each, naming a specific moment from this unit. Short or generic responses are returned for revision.

Concrete Experience

Describe one specific moment from this unit — a lab, a NETLAB session, a ticket, or your own equipment. Not a summary; one moment. What were you doing, and what happened?

Reflective Observation

What surprised you? Name something you assumed about networks, the OSI model, or cabling and were surprised to discover. Include anything you expected to be difficult that was not, or the reverse.

Abstract Conceptualization

Connect that moment to the model. Which concept from the reading or the videos explains what you saw, and how?

Active Experimentation

What will you do differently next time — in the next unit, on the exam, or on the job — because of this? Be specific.
