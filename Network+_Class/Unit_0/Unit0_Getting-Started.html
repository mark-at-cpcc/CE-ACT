0.1 — Course Disclaimers (Read First)

Everything below is here because a student somewhere was surprised by it. None of it is fine print, and all of it is short.

No Guarantee of Certification

This course prepares you to sit the CompTIA Network+ (N10-009) certification exam. It does not certify you, and no instructor, institution, or training provider can guarantee a passing result. The exam is administered by CompTIA, scheduled and paid for separately through Pearson VUE, and scored independently.

What determines your outcome is the work you put in over these six weeks. Students who complete the reading, watch the videos, do the labs, and reach the practice-exam threshold pass at a high rate. Students who skip the out-of-class work generally do not, regardless of how well class went.

The Official Objectives Are the Final Authority

Course materials, textbook content, and video resources are all interpretations of CompTIA’s published exam objectives. Where anything in this course conflicts with the official N10-009 objectives document, the official document wins. Download it free from comptia.org and treat it as your master checklist.

In a few places your textbook uses different words than CompTIA does for the same thing. On course pages you will see CompTIA’s term first, with the textbook’s term right after it in parentheses — for example, Secure Access Service Edge (Secure Access Secure Edge). Quizzes and tests use CompTIA’s term only, because that is what the real exam uses.

Exam objectives are periodically revised. If CompTIA updates the N10-009 objectives during our term, we adjust; the certification is a moving target by design.

Hands-On Work Carries Real Risk

This course involves hands-on work with live network equipment — switches, routers, access points, patch panels, and the cabling between them — and with the network commands and capture tools technicians use every day. Treat every device as powered unless you have confirmed otherwise, keep fingers and tools clear of power supplies and fans, and never run a scan, a capture, or a configuration change against a network you do not own or have written permission to test. Doing so to someone else’s network can be a crime, and “it was for class” is not a defense. Everything in this course can be practiced on the lab pods, the classroom gear, and your own home network.

0.2 — What This Course Covers

CompTIA Network+ is the industry’s vendor-neutral certification for the person who makes networks work: the technician who installs the switch, assigns the addresses, configures the wireless, watches the monitoring console, and gets the call when “the internet is down.” It is the credential that help-desk, field-service, and junior network-administration jobs ask for by name, and the foundation that Security+, Cisco, and cloud certifications assume you already have.

This course takes you from little or no networking background to a first-attempt pass on N10-009 in six weeks. That is an aggressive pace, and the course is built for it: every week pairs reading from the Sybex Study Guide with Professor Messer’s videos, graded hands-on labs on real equipment, daily trouble tickets, and practice questions you repeat until they are perfect. You will finish knowing how a network is built, how it is run, how it is attacked, and how it is fixed — and you will have the exam-readiness evidence to prove it.

CompTIA Network+ Passes at 720

The exam is scored on a 100–900 scale and passes at 720. Because the scale starts at 100 rather than zero, 720 works out to about 78% of raw points. That is a high bar: a student who treats 70% on a practice set as “good enough” fails the real exam. This course’s mastery gate of 800 exists so that your exam day has a cushion.

0.4 — What You're Expected to Do

Every unit runs the same way, so by the second week it will feel routine. On Tuesday you meet the problem before you have the words for it — a dead link light, a workstation with a strange address, a Wi-Fi network that works in one room and not the next. On Wednesday we name it: the model, the terminology, the why. On Thursday you apply it — a lab, a ticket, and a quick exit check so you and I both know where you stand.

Outside class, each unit has the same ten parts, numbered the same way every time: the pre-assessment you take on your own before the reading (expect to miss most of it — that is the point), the reading, the videos, a take-home lab on your own equipment, the practice set you repeat until it is perfect, the NETLAB labs on real equipment, a reflection on what you actually learned, the unit test, and three trouble tickets, one per class day. Do them in that order. The order is the method.

If you already own a laptop or desktop, plan to use it for this class. You need a full Windows, macOS, or Linux machine: the NETLAB pods run in a browser, but the take-home labs use your own command line (ipconfig, ping, tracert, nslookup and their equivalents) and free tools such as Wireshark, which Chromebooks and tablets cannot run. 0.10 — Using NETLAB

Getting In

  1. Always launch from Brightspace. Open the NETLAB — Student Labs folder under Coursework and click the link for the lab you are starting. Launching from the course shell is what associates your session with this class.
  2. Make a reservation. Choose a date and time window; NETLAB allocates a pod to you for exactly that period.
  3. Read the worksheet before your window opens. Reading during your reservation burns time you paid for.
  4. Complete the lab in one continuous session, capturing screenshots as you go.
  5. Submit your screenshots to the matching lab quiz in Brightspace.

Screenshots — How Labs Get Graded

Each lab has its own screenshot quiz. Every screenshot must show the NETLAB timestamp and your reservation ID. This course uses the NDG Network+ series on the NDG Network+ Pod: real routers, switches, and hosts, not simulations. A reservation can be extended thirty minutes at a time from the link at the top right of the reservation window, as long as the pod is not booked next.

One Session, No Save Point

If your reservation expires mid-lab, the environment resets and you begin again from step one. Book generously — if a lab is estimated at 1.25 hours, reserve closer to 2.
