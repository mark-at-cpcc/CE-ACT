Course Overview and Introduction

What this course is designed to accomplish, and what a student who completes it will be able to do.

Course Overview

This course introduces the design, implementation, operation, security, and troubleshooting of wired and wireless computer networks, from the physical media up. Students learn the models, media, addressing, routing, switching, and services that make modern networks work, and practice the monitoring, documentation, and structured troubleshooting that keep them working. The goal of the course is to pass the CompTIA Network+ (N10-009) certification exam on the first attempt.

Course Objectives

Broad, instructor-facing statements of what the course as a whole is designed to accomplish.

  1. Prepare students with little or no prior networking experience to comprehend, articulate, and apply the networking concepts, implementation skills, operational practices, security controls, and troubleshooting methods required by the CompTIA Network+ (N10-009) certification exam.
  2. Deliver instruction aligned to all five N10-009 exam domains — Networking Concepts, Network Implementation, Network Operations, Network Security, and Network Troubleshooting — in proportion to their weighting on the official exam.
  3. Build technical competency through direct, hands-on experience with live routed, switched, and wireless networks — on the NDG Network+ lab pod, on classroom networking hardware, and in network simulation — rather than through lecture alone.
  4. Establish CompTIA’s seven-step network troubleshooting methodology as a structured, repeatable process that students apply consistently to cabling, addressing, routing, switching, wireless, service, and performance problems throughout the course.
  5. Instill professional operational practice — accurate network documentation, change management, configuration backup, and ethical, authorized use of network tools such as packet capture and port scanners — as habits, not topics.
  6. Assess readiness for certification through progressively rigorous evaluation, culminating in a scaled practice-exam threshold that predicts a first-attempt pass on the official N10-009 exam.

Student Learning Outcomes

Specific, measurable statements of what a student who completes the course will be able to do.

Upon successful completion of this course, the student will be able to:

  1. Explain the OSI and TCP/IP models and use them to describe how data moves across a network; identify transmission media, connectors, and transceivers by inspection; and compare network topologies, architectures, appliances, and cloud connectivity options.
  2. Design and apply IPv4 addressing schemes, including classless subnetting, and describe IPv6 addressing; configure and verify static and dynamic routing, VLANs, trunking, spanning tree, and wireless networks in live lab and simulated environments.
  3. Implement and monitor core network services — DHCP, DNS, and time services — and use SNMP, syslog, flow data, and packet capture to assess network health; maintain network documentation and explain disaster-recovery and high-availability concepts.
  4. Identify common network attacks from their symptoms and apply appropriate hardening, segmentation, access-control, and authentication measures to defend a network.
  5. Apply CompTIA’s seven-step troubleshooting methodology, using the appropriate software commands and hardware tools, to diagnose and resolve cabling, interface, service, and performance problems presented as realistic network scenarios.
  6. Demonstrate exam readiness for the CompTIA Network+ (N10-009) certification by achieving a scaled score of 800 or higher on two consecutive full-length practice examinations.
