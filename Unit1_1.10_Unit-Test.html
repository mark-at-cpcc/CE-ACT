Why Troubleshooting Carries the Most Weight After Hardware

Domain 5.0 is 28% of your certification exam — the largest single domain. Rather than saving it for the end, every unit tests the troubleshooting that matches its own hardware. You will have diagnosed power and memory faults many times before you sit the real exam.

What This Score Is Actually For

Your unit test score does not move your grade. It moves your study plan.

Because each attempt draws from all four objectives, a low score on any one attempt can simply mean an unlucky draw of harder items — reset and take it again before drawing conclusions. If a pattern holds across two or three attempts, that is the signal worth acting on, and that is where your Week 2 review time should go.

The score that eventually matters is the full-length practice exam, where you need 85 raw points out of 100 — a 780 scaled — on two consecutive attempts before I will recommend you schedule the real exam. The cut score on 220-1201 is 675 out of 900, which works out to roughly 72% of raw points. The margin between 780 and 675 is deliberate: it absorbs test-day nerves and unfamiliar phrasing.

How to Prepare

  1. Work the Section 1.7 practice set and identify every confident-and-wrong item
  2. Complete both NETLAB labs in Section 1.8 — they are the closest thing to performance-based exam practice you will get
  3. Re-read the Exam Essentials for Chapter 1, and the assigned portions of Chapters 2 and 12
  4. Complete the Chapter 1 Review Questions and both Performance-Based Questions
  5. Rewatch Messer 5.1 Troubleshooting Hardware — it is the densest exam material of the week
  6. Recite the six troubleshooting steps in order, from memory, without looking
If You Score Below 70% Across Multiple Attempts

Email me before Week 2 begins. Not as a formality — Unit 2 builds directly on this material, and a gap here widens fast in an accelerated course. Catching it in Week 1 costs an hour. Catching it in Week 4 costs the certification.
