Technical Overview

NETLAB+ is a remotely accessible virtualization platform that provisions dedicated physical and virtual lab equipment to scheduled student sessions. Access is reservation-based: a pod of resources is allocated to you for a fixed window, and the environment is reset to a clean baseline when that window closes. The NDG A+ v5 lab series maps directly to CompTIA A+ exam objectives and provides guided procedures against live systems rather than simulations.

In Plain Terms

NETLAB gives you a real computer to work on through your browser. You book a time slot, the system hands you a machine for that window, and when time is up it wipes it clean for the next person. Nothing you do can break anything permanently, which is exactly why it is the safest place to learn.

Why These Labs Matter More Than They Look

Roughly a quarter of the Core 1 exam is Hardware and another 28% is Troubleshooting. Both are assessed partly through performance-based questions — items that ask you to do something in a simulated environment rather than recognize a correct sentence.

Reading about a device manager and navigating one under a countdown are different skills, and the exam tests the second. Students who complete every NETLAB lab consistently report the performance-based questions felt familiar. Students who skipped them report the opposite, usually afterward.

These labs are also the only place in this course where you work on a live system with no risk of breaking something that matters. Use that. Click things. Look around beyond the numbered steps.

How to Access NETLAB

Complete access instructions, including first-time login and reservation walkthroughs, are in Unit 0, Section 0.10. The short version:

  1. Open the lab link in Brightspace. Each lab has its own link inside this unit. Do not go directly to the NETLAB site — launch from the course shell so your session is associated with this class.
  2. Make a reservation. You select a date and time window. The system allocates a pod of equipment to you for that period.
  3. Open the lab worksheet first. Worksheets are in the NETLAB — Lab Worksheets folder under Coursework, and inside NETLAB itself. Read it before your reservation begins, not during.
  4. Complete the lab in a single sitting and capture every required screenshot as you go.
  5. Submit your screenshots to the matching lab quiz in Brightspace.
You Get One Continuous Session

If your reservation time runs out, the lab resets and you start over from the beginning. There is no pause, no save state, and no partial progress carried forward.

Book a window longer than you think you need. If a lab is estimated at 1.25 hours, reserve closer to 2. The extra time costs you nothing if you finish early.

Do Not Wait Until the Deadline

There are a limited number of lab pods assigned to this course. When everyone books the same Sunday evening, the slots are gone and there is nothing I can do about it — the resources are finite and the deadline does not move because you could not get a reservation.

Reserve your time early in the week. This is the single most common avoidable way students lose points in this course.

Screenshot Requirements

Screenshots are how the lab gets graded, so the mechanics matter. Improperly submitted screenshots cannot receive credit or partial credit.

Capture As You Go, Not At The End

Take each screenshot at the moment the worksheet asks for it. Students who plan to "go back and grab them at the end" discover the environment has moved on, the state is gone, and the only way to recover it is to run the entire lab again on a new reservation.

Lab Tools Stay Inside NETLAB

Do not attempt these lab procedures on the CPCC network, on college equipment, or on any network or system you do not own. Tools and techniques used in a lab environment may violate federal or state law, or institutional acceptable use policy, when applied elsewhere.

The NETLAB virtual machines exist precisely so you have a lawful, isolated place to practice. Use them, and only them. You are solely responsible for compliance if you take these techniques outside that environment.

Before You Start

  • Complete the Unit 1 instructional material first — Sections 1.3, 1.4, and 1.5. The labs assume you know the vocabulary.
  • Download and read the lab worksheet before your reservation window opens
  • Reserve a block longer than the estimated time
  • Have a reliable internet connection — a dropped connection during a session costs you the session
  • Know your screenshot tool before you start, not during
Completion Standard — 100 Points Each

Full credit requires every screenshot the worksheet specifies, pasted into the correct field of the lab quiz, with the NETLAB timestamp and reservation visible. Submissions missing screenshots or with unreadable captures are returned for revision and resubmitted for full credit.

You may reattempt the labs as many times as you wish before the deadline — each attempt requires a new reservation.
