Welcome Announcement

Welcome to CompTIA A+ Core 1

Six weeks from now, you'll be ready to sit a real IT certification exam. Here's how we get there — and why you're going to be good at this.

Welcome Aboard

In a few days you're going to do something most people never do: open a computer, understand what's inside it, and learn to fix it when it breaks. Six weeks from now you'll walk into a testing center and sit for a real industry certification — the CompTIA A+ Core 1 exam — and you'll be ready for it.

I've built this course to take you from zero computer experience to exam-ready in six weeks. If you've never opened a case, never known what RAM stood for, never had a reason to care — that's exactly who this course was written for. Nobody in this room is expected to already know this stuff. That's the whole point of showing up.

In Plain Terms

You'll learn what's inside a computer, how computers talk to each other, and how to figure out what's wrong when one stops working. That's it. That's the course.

What Makes This Different

This isn't a lecture course where you read about hardware and hope it sticks. Every week follows the same rhythm: you touch it before we name it. You'll open a case Wednesday morning and identify parts by reasoning about what they connect to — then we give you the vocabulary in the afternoon, once you already have something to hang it on. Thursday, you apply it.

Why This Works

Adults learn best when they know why something matters before they're asked to memorize it. Students who get the terminology first, before any hands-on context, tend to watch it slide right off. Students who've already looked at a DIMM slot have somewhere to put the word "DIMM" when it shows up on the slide. That's not a teaching gimmick — it's the whole design.

What You're Working Toward

Everything in this course maps to the official CompTIA A+ Core 1 exam objectives — hardware, mobile devices, networking, virtualization and cloud, and the troubleshooting skills that tie it all together. By the end of Week 6, you'll know exactly where you stand, because we track it every single week.

A Number Worth Knowing Now

The exam is scored 100 to 900, and you need 675 to pass. Because the scale starts at 100 instead of zero, that's actually about 72% of raw points — not 75%. We'll say this more than once. It's the kind of thing that quietly costs people a passing score if nobody tells them.
