We Are Reading Chapters 3 and 10 Out of Order

The book is organized by topic. This course is organized by when you need each skill. Installing Windows belongs right next to learning what Windows editions exist and what hardware they need. How to Read a Certification Textbook

You learned this method in Core 1, and it works the same way here. If you sit down and read Chapter 1 straight through, you will finish with a warm sense of familiarity and retain very little.

PassWhat You DoTime
1. SurveyRead the chapter's Exam Essentials first. It tells you what the chapter thinks matters, so you read looking for something.10 min
2. ReadWork through the sections. Stop at every screenshot and table — Windows chapters show you exactly where settings live, and the exam asks about those locations.Bulk of your time
3. RetrieveClose the book. Write down what you remember from the section before moving to the next one. This feels unpleasant and is the step that actually builds memory.5 min per section
4. TestDo the Review Questions without looking back. Mark every answer you guessed, even the ones you got right.20–30 min
5. RepairFor each miss and each lucky guess, go find the passage that explains it.15–20 min

Do the Performance-Based Questions

Every chapter in Volume 2 ends with one Performance-Based Question — ten across the volume. This week you work Chapter 1's.

Performance-based questions on the real exam ask you to do something — put steps in order, match a setting to a task, configure an option — rather than recognize a correct sentence. You practiced these in Core 1. Keep doing them; they are still the pages most worth your time.

Note-Taking That Earns Its Time

Copying sentences from the book into a notebook feels productive and does almost nothing. Try these instead:

  • Comparison tables. Whenever the book distinguishes two similar things — FAT32 vs. exFAT, Home vs. Pro, clean install vs. upgrade, MBR vs. GPT — build a two-column table in your own words. The exam lives in these distinctions.
  • Write the question, not the answer. "Which Windows editions can join a domain?" on one side, the answer on the other. You have made a flashcard and thought about what is testable.
  • Keep a "where does it live" list. Every time the book names a Windows setting, write down where you find it. Core 2 asks about locations constantly.
  • Start a ticket phrase bank. When the documentation section shows a clear issue description or resolution, copy the pattern, not the words. You will use it in every Ticket of the Day.
Read With the Machine in Front of You

Do this week's reading beside your own computer. When the book describes Windows editions, open Settings, then System, then About and find your edition. When it describes file systems, open File Explorer, right-click a drive, and choose Properties. Seeing it on your own machine turns a paragraph into something you remember.
