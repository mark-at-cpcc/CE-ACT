Instructions — Read Before You Begin

What This Is

Three help-desk tickets, one for each class meeting this unit. Work each one on your own. Every ticket is a real kind of call, with real evidence, and three questions that ask what a technician would actually do next. Plan on about 20 minutes per ticket.

How to Work a Ticket

  1. Read the call, then stop. Before you look at any evidence, write one sentence in your notes: what do you think is wrong, and how sure are you? You will come back to it.
  2. Study the exhibits as if you had just collected them from the user's computer.
  3. Answer the three questions. Each one stands on its own. You get one attempt per question.
  4. Write your own progress note, then compare it with the Ticket Solution.
  5. Look back. The reflection prompts sit inside the Ticket Solution, below the solution itself. Answer them in your notes. They are about your thinking, not the content, and nobody else will see them.

What You Will See When You Answer

  • A flag that says Correct or Incorrect — nothing more. You will not be shown which answer was right.
  • If you miss one, go back to the exhibit and the call and work out why. That search is the lesson.
  • Answer all three correctly and the Ticket Solution opens on its own. Otherwise, click Ticket Solution to open it yourself — after you have written your note. The Look Back prompts are inside it.
  • Reset These Questions clears a ticket so you can work it again.

About the Exhibits

Each ticket includes exhibits labeled A, B, and C. Every exhibit is representative of the output from the computer being troubleshot — what you would see on that machine's screen if you opened the tool or settings window, or ran the command shown. Treat them as evidence you collected yourself during the call.

They are simplified to show what matters, the same way a performance-based question on the 220-1202 exam presents a tool or a command's output. Read them the way a technician reads a screen: column headings first, then the values that stand out, then the details that do not fit.

The Method Behind Every Ticket

Each question is tagged with the step of CompTIA's troubleshooting method it exercises. You will use this method for the rest of your career; these tickets are where it becomes a habit.

  1. Identify the problem
  2. Establish a theory of probable cause
  3. Test the theory to determine the cause
  4. Establish a plan of action to resolve the problem and implement the solution
  5. Verify full system functionality and, if applicable, implement preventive measures
  6. Document findings, actions, and outcomes

About the Times and Names

Every person, company, and device in these tickets is fictional. Times shown inside a ticket are part of the scenario, not course deadlines — course due dates are always in the Course Schedule.

  2. Angela said nobody ever tells her what is wrong. Write the two sentences you would say to her, with no jargon, that would have changed how this month felt for her.
  3. Restarting worked every time — for a few days. Why is "it works now" not the same as "it is fixed"? Where else do you accept "it works now"?
  4. Rewrite HD-1-0811 as it should have been written. What is the one sentence that would have saved everyone after R. Tan the most time?
Ticket 2 of 3 Five New Laptops and a 6 GB Video Wednesday · Objectives 1.1, 1.2, and 1.3 — Operating systems, installation, and editions · About 20 minutes
Ticket HD-1-0915
PriorityMedium — business requirements unmet on new equipment
CategoryOperating system · Configuration
CallerLuis Ortega, Owner, Ortega Landscape Design (6 staff)
DeviceAssets OLD-LT-01 to OLD-LT-05 · Five new laptops, bought at a retail store last week
The Call

"We bought five new laptops for the office. Two problems. My accountant says our files have to be encrypted and the laptops have to join the domain your company set up for us — but there's no option for either one. And I can't copy our client presentation video, about 6 gigabytes, onto the USB drive I take to meetings. It says the file is too large for the destination. The drive is 64 gigabytes and it's empty!"

You learn that the laptops came with the operating system the store preinstalled, and that the USB drive was bought years ago and formatted on an older computer. Luis needs every application already installed on the laptops to stay where it is.

Write your own progress note and resolution first — and say whether the two problems turned out to be related. Then open the Ticket Solution below to compare.

Ticket Solution

Reported: five new laptops cannot join the domain or encrypt drives; a 6 GB video will not copy to an empty 64 GB USB drive.

Found: laptops run Windows 11 Home, which supports neither domain joining nor BitLocker. USB drive formatted FAT32, which limits single files to 4 GB. The two problems are unrelated.

Action: confirmed the USB drive was empty, reformatted it as exFAT, and copied the video successfully. Upgraded each laptop in place to Windows 11 Pro with purchased Pro licenses; installed applications and files preserved. Joined all five to the domain and enabled BitLocker; recovery keys stored in the company's key escrow.

Preventive: recommended that future laptop purchases specify Windows 11 Pro; noted in the client record.

Look Back

  1. Did you think the two problems were related? They were not. Why does checking that matter before you start fixing?
  2. Luis bought the cheaper edition at a store. How do you explain the extra license cost without making him feel he made a bad decision?
  3. "64 GB and it's empty" made the error sound impossible. What other "impossible" errors have you met that were really a rule you did not know yet?
  4. BitLocker is only as good as its recovery key. Where should these keys be kept, and who should be able to reach them?
Ticket 3 of 3 The New Hire's First Day Thursday · Objectives 1.10, 1.11, and 4.7 — Applications, cloud tools, and professionalism · About 20 minutes

Write your own progress note and resolution first — including what you told Tasha, and the problem you could not fix today. Then open the Ticket Solution below to compare.

Ticket Solution

Reported: new employee unable to install the design application, open company email, or keep working because of a nearly full disk, on his first day.

Found: application requires dedicated graphics with 4 GB VRAM; RA-LT-31 has integrated graphics, so it cannot run the application in a supported way. No license assigned to the new account. Cloud storage syncing the entire 184 GB Projects library locally; disk 97% full.

Action: with the HR manager's approval, assigned the email license; mail opens. Changed sync so files download only when opened and limited it to his project folders; disk at 31%. Escalated a request for a workstation that meets the application's requirements.

Communication: told the HR manager and the employee plainly that email and storage were fixed today, and that the design application needs different hardware — with a realistic date for it.

Preventive: the laptop was reassigned without being checked against the new role. Recommended adding a requirements check to the onboarding checklist.

Look Back

  1. Tasha asked, "What do I tell him?" Write the two sentences you would give her — honest about the timeline, with no blame on anyone.
  2. Which problem did you choose to fix first? Was it the one with the most business impact, or the one you knew how to fix?
  3. This laptop was reassigned without anyone checking it against the new job. Which document should have caught that, and what line would you add to it?
  4. You had to tell a new employee his laptop cannot run his main tool. How do you deliver bad news so the person leaves knowing what happens next?
What These Tickets Give You
  • Reading a ticket history the way the next technician will, and writing notes that do not leave them guessing
  • Matching a problem to a rule you did not know yet: file system limits, Windows edition features, and application requirements
  • Setting expectations honestly with a frustrated caller and a new employee
  • Progress notes another technician could pick up without asking you a single question
