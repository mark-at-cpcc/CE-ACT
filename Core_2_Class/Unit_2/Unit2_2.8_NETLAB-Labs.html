Three labs this unit, roughly 45 minutes to 1.25 hours each. These are required and they are graded.

Three Labs Again — Reserve Early

This course has a finite number of lab pods, and Unit 2 asks for three sessions. When the whole class books the same Sunday night, the slots are simply gone, and the deadline does not move because a reservation was unavailable.

Book all three windows at the start of the unit.

Getting In

  1. Always launch from Brightspace. Go to the NETLAB — Student Labs folder under Coursework and click the link for the specific lab you're starting — do not navigate to the NETLAB site directly. Launching from the course shell is what associates your session with this class and your roster entry.
  2. Make a reservation. Choose a date and time window. NETLAB allocates a pod to you for exactly that period.
  3. Read the worksheet before your window opens. Worksheets live in the NETLAB — Lab Worksheets folder under Coursework in Brightspace, and inside NETLAB itself. Reading during your reservation burns time you paid for.
  4. Complete the lab in one continuous session, capturing screenshots as you go.
  5. Submit your screenshots to the matching lab quiz in Brightspace — each lab's screenshots go to that lab's own quiz.
One Session, No Save Point

If your reservation expires mid-lab, the environment resets and you begin again from step one. There is no pause, no saved progress, and no partial completion.

Book generously. If a lab is estimated at 1.25 hours, reserve closer to 2. Finishing early costs you nothing; running out costs you the entire attempt.

Lab 05 Includes a Little Linux

Lab 05 runs the same kinds of tasks in a Windows command prompt and in a Linux terminal. We study Linux properly in Unit 3, so do not worry that you have not covered it yet — the worksheet walks you through every Linux command step by step. Pay attention to how similar the two feel. Meeting something before we name it is how this course is built, and it will make Unit 3 easier.

Run It Safely Here, Not at Home

NETLAB is where you run the commands Section 2.4 told you not to try on your own machine. The pod is wiped when your session ends, so nothing you do inside it can cost you anything. If a lab has you format a volume or work in diskpart, this is exactly the right place to do it.

Screenshots Are How This Gets Graded

Each lab has its own quiz in Brightspace, and each lab's screenshots go into that lab's own quiz — not attached as separate files, and not combined into one submission. Paste each screenshot directly into its assigned question field.

Every screenshot must show the full screen, including the timestamp and your reservation ID. A cropped image that could have come from anywhere is not proof that you did the lab.

Capture the Output, Not Just the Screen

These three labs cover material you will see on the unit test and on the exam. When a lab has you run ipconfig or open Event Viewer, write down what you actually see — the address, the Event ID, the error text. Two weeks from now, in Unit 4, you will be asked to recognize those same outputs in a scenario question, and the version you wrote down yourself is the one you will remember.
