Instructions — Read Before You Begin

What This Is

Three help-desk tickets, one for each class meeting this unit. Work each one on your own. Every ticket is a real kind of call, with real evidence, and three questions that ask what a technician would actually do next. Plan on about 20 minutes per ticket.

How to Work a Ticket

  1. Read the call, then stop. Before you look at any evidence, write one sentence in your notes: what do you think is wrong, and how sure are you? You will come back to it.
  2. Study the exhibits as if you had just collected them from the user's computer.
  3. Answer the three questions. Each one stands on its own. You get one attempt per question.
  4. Write your own progress note, then compare it with the Ticket Solution.
  5. Look back. The reflection prompts sit inside the Ticket Solution, below the solution itself. Answer them in your notes. They are about your thinking, not the content, and nobody else will see them.

What You Will See When You Answer

  • A flag that says Correct or Incorrect — nothing more. You will not be shown which answer was right.
  • If you miss one, go back to the exhibit and the call and work out why. That search is the lesson.
  • Answer all three correctly and the Ticket Solution opens on its own. Otherwise, click Ticket Solution to open it yourself — after you have written your note. The Look Back prompts are inside it.
  • Reset These Questions clears a ticket so you can work it again.

About the Exhibits

Each ticket includes exhibits labeled A, B, and C. Every exhibit is representative of the output from the computer being troubleshot — what you would see on that machine's screen if you opened the tool or settings window, or ran the command shown. Treat them as evidence you collected yourself during the call.

They are simplified to show what matters, the same way a performance-based question on the 220-1202 exam presents a tool or a command's output. Read them the way a technician reads a screen: column headings first, then the values that stand out, then the details that do not fit.

The Method Behind Every Ticket

Each question is tagged with the step of CompTIA's troubleshooting method it exercises. You will use this method for the rest of your career; these tickets are where it becomes a habit.

  1. Identify the problem
  2. Establish a theory of probable cause
  3. Test the theory to determine the cause
  4. Establish a plan of action to resolve the problem and implement the solution
  5. Verify full system functionality and, if applicable, implement preventive measures
  6. Document findings, actions, and outcomes

About the Times and Names

Every person, company, and device in these tickets is fictional. Times shown inside a ticket are part of the scenario, not course deadlines — course due dates are always in the Course Schedule.

Ticket 1 of 3 "It's a Virus. It Has to Be." Tuesday · Objective 1.4 — Windows features and tools · About 20 minutes
Ticket HD-2-0412
PriorityMedium — one user; a workaround exists
CategoryWorkstation performance
CallerDenise Alvarez, Accounts Payable Lead, Hollister & Reyes CPA (14 staff)
DeviceAsset CPA-WS-07 · Desktop · Windows 11 Pro · Core i5 · 8 GB RAM · 1 TB 7200 RPM hard disk drive
The Call

"Every morning since last Monday my computer is useless from about nine until almost ten. The mouse jerks across the screen, QuickBooks says Not Responding, and once a box popped up in the corner that I'd never seen before. It's a virus. It has to be. I restarted it twice yesterday and that made it worse. By ten-thirty it's fine, so I've been coming in at seven to get my work done before it starts — but month-end close is Thursday and I can't keep losing an hour a day."

While you ask your questions, you learn three more things. Last week the office manager, Kevin Morales, installed a new cloud backup product called CloudVault, because the firm's insurance carrier now requires a daily offsite backup. Nobody mentioned it to Denise. A full antivirus scan ran overnight and found nothing.

Close the Ticket

Write your own progress note and resolution in your notes first — device, what you were asked to determine, what you found in order, what you did, and what is still open. Then open the Ticket Solution below to compare. If you answered all three questions correctly, it opens on its own.

Ticket Solution

Reported: severe slowdown roughly 9:00–9:45 daily since Monday; caller suspected a virus. Overnight antivirus scan clean; the unfamiliar pop-up was CloudVault's progress notification.

Found: Task Manager at 9:14 — disk 100%, CloudVault Backup Agent 31.6 MB/s, CPU 31%, memory 78%. Resource Monitor confirmed CloudVault reading the QuickBooks company files and user profile. Task Scheduler: "CloudVault Daily Full" triggered at 9:00 AM daily, configured last week by K. Morales.

Action: with K. Morales's approval, moved the trigger to 7:00 PM daily. Explained the cause to the caller. Will confirm tonight's run completed.

Separate finding — open: System log shows recurring disk Event ID 153 (I/O retried) beginning 23 days ago, before CloudVault was installed. Recommended a drive health check and replacement planning; flagged to K. Morales. Priority: confirm the backup completes successfully before the drive is examined.

Look Back

  1. Return to the sentence you wrote before the exhibits. How close was it? If you also suspected a virus, what made it convincing — the evidence, or Denise's certainty?
  2. Which single exhibit changed your thinking most, and why that one rather than the others?
  3. Question 3 asked you to notice a date. Did you? Technicians who stop at the first cause that fits miss the second one. Where, in your own work or study, do you stop at the first explanation that fits?
  4. Denise has been coming in at seven for a week because of a schedule someone else set. How will you explain the cause so she does not feel foolish for calling it a virus — and so Kevin does not feel blamed for setting it?
Ticket 2 of 3 Three Complaints, One Laptop Wednesday · Objective 1.6 — Windows settings · About 20 minutes
Ticket HD-2-0418
PriorityHigh — a security near-miss, plus a mobile user who cannot work reliably
CategoryWorkstation configuration · Security
CallerPriya Natarajan, Regional Sales Representative, Carolina Medical Supply
DeviceAsset CMS-LT-112 · Laptop · Windows 11 Pro · 16 GB RAM · 512 GB NVMe solid-state drive
The Call

"I have three problems and I honestly don't know if they're related. The first one scared me. I got an invoice from a supplier we really do use, I double-clicked it, and the antivirus screamed. Security says it was a program, not a PDF — but it said .pdf. I checked! Second, I close the lid between appointments, and when I open it two hours later the battery's dead and the bag is hot. Third, my presentation clicker stops working if I pause on a slide for a few minutes. I have to unplug that little USB receiver and plug it back in, right in front of the client."

Ticket Solution

Reported: three issues since the laptop was reimaged two weeks ago — a near-miss malicious attachment, a battery drained and the laptop hot after closing the lid, and a USB presentation receiver that stops responding when idle on battery.

Found and fixed: (1) "Hide extensions for known file types" enabled; the attachment was Q3_Invoice_CMS-4471.pdf.exe, quarantined by antivirus. Enabled extension display; reported the message to security. (2) Lid action on battery set to Do nothing, so the laptop kept running in the bag. Set to Hibernate on battery. (3) USB selective suspend enabled on battery, suspending the idle receiver. Disabled on battery. Verified all three with the caller.

Root cause and escalation: all three settings came from the new standard image. Escalated to the imaging team to correct the image, so the remaining laptops built from it are fixed before their users call with the same problems.

Look Back

  1. Return to your answer about whether the problems were related. What is the actual connection between them, and why does it matter more than any single fix?
  2. Question 2 set three requirements that all had to be met. Did you read every one of them before choosing? On the exam, the deciding constraint is often in the final sentence.
  3. Priya said "I checked!" — and she did. The system misled her. How do you tell her it was not her fault, while still teaching her the habit that protects her next time?
  4. Fixing her laptop takes ten minutes; fixing the image protects forty people. Where else might you be treating one symptom when the real fix is upstream?
Ticket 3 of 3 Monday Morning After the Server Move Thursday · Objective 1.5 — Microsoft command-line tools · About 20 minutes
Ticket HD-2-0426
PriorityHigh — nine users at one branch cannot reach a shared drive they need today
CategoryFile access · Network
CallerMarcus Bell, Branch Manager, Greenway Property Management — Gastonia branch
DeviceAsset GPM-GA-03 · Desktop · Windows 11 Pro · joined to the GREENWAY domain
Your accessA remote support session with an administrator command prompt. Nobody can be on site before noon.
The Call

"Nobody here can open the S: drive. It's got a red X on it. We have three lease closings today and every file is on that drive. Your team moved the server this weekend — was that you? Because I need this fixed now, not at noon."

The change record confirms it. Over the weekend the infrastructure team retired the old file server, FS01, and moved its shares to a new server, FS02, at 10.20.1.25. They published a new Group Policy object, Branch Drive Maps v2, which maps S: to \\FS02\shared, and asked for a DNS alias so the old name would keep working during the transition. Branch computers were powered off all weekend.

Stop Here

Marcus is frustrated, and some of it is aimed at your team. Before the exhibits, write two things in your notes: your first theory, and the first sentence you would say to Marcus.

Write your own progress note and resolution first — including what happens to the other eight users, and what you recommend so the next migration does not cause the same morning. Then open the Ticket Solution below to compare.

Ticket Solution

Reported: S: unavailable for all nine Gastonia users following the weekend FS01-to-FS02 migration. Lease closings today.

Found: remote session to GPM-GA-03. net use — S: mapped to \\FS01\shared, Unavailable; H: on HQ-FS03 OK, so the WAN link and authentication are working. ping FS01 — name not found; ping 10.20.1.25 (FS02) — 0% loss. nslookup FS01 — no record returned by the branch DNS server; the requested alias is not in place. Escalated the missing alias to infrastructure. gpresult /r — user policy last applied Friday; Branch Drive Maps v2 not applied.

Action: net use S: /delete, then gpupdate /force; caller signed out and back in. S: now maps to \\FS02\shared and lease files open. Confirmed with the caller.

Remaining and preventive: sent sign-out/sign-in instructions for the other eight users. Recommended that future migrations verify the DNS alias at each branch and confirm the new policy applies before the old server is retired.

Look Back

  1. Marcus opened with "was that you?" Look at the first sentence you wrote. Did it defend your team, or did it start fixing his morning? Was he right?
  2. Exhibit A held a line most people skip: the H: drive was OK. What did that single line rule out, and how many minutes would you have spent without noticing it?
  3. Question 1 offered several commands that delete, format, or clean. On a real call, under pressure, any of them can look like a quick fix. What is your rule, starting today, before running a command like that against anything you cannot see?
  4. You fixed one computer. Eight more users have the same problem, and the next migration will cause it again. Who needs to hear what from you — and which of those conversations is the hardest one?
What These Tickets Give You
  • Practice reading real evidence — Task Manager, Event Viewer, Task Scheduler, Power Options, File Explorer, and command output — the way a performance-based question presents it
  • The habit of committing to a theory before the evidence, then checking honestly whether you were right
  • The least disruptive fix, chosen deliberately, and the second problem most people miss
  • The instinct to stop before running a destructive command against something you cannot see
  • Progress notes another technician could pick up without asking you a single question
