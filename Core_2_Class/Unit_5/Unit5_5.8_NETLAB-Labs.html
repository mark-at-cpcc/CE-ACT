Three labs this unit, roughly 45 minutes to 1.25 hours each. These are required and they are graded.

Three Labs, and a Practice Exam

Unit 5 carries three NETLAB labs alongside the first full-length practice exam. That is the busiest assessment week of the term even though the reading is lighter. Reserve all three lab windows at the start of the unit, and leave the practice exam an uninterrupted block of its own.

Getting In

  1. Always launch from Brightspace. Go to the NETLAB — Student Labs folder under Coursework and click the link for the specific lab you're starting — do not navigate to the NETLAB site directly. Launching from the course shell is what associates your session with this class and your roster entry.
  2. Make a reservation. Choose a date and time window. NETLAB allocates a pod to you for exactly that period.
  3. Read the worksheet before your window opens. Worksheets live in the NETLAB — Lab Worksheets folder under Coursework in Brightspace, and inside NETLAB itself.
  4. Complete the lab in one continuous session, capturing screenshots as you go.
  5. Submit your screenshots to the matching lab quiz in Brightspace — each lab's screenshots go to that lab's own quiz.
One Session, No Save Point

If your reservation expires mid-lab, the environment resets and you begin again from step one. There is no pause, no saved progress, and no partial completion.

Book generously. If a lab is estimated at 1.25 hours, reserve closer to 2. Finishing early costs you nothing; running out costs you the entire attempt.

Lab 10 Is the Permissions Question, Answered by Doing It

Section 5.3 tells you that when NTFS and share permissions disagree, the more restrictive one wins. Lab 10 is where you set both and watch it happen.

As you work, deliberately create the mismatch: give the share Full Control and the folder Read, then try to save a file from the other machine. The error message you get is the one behind a ticket you will work someday, and having produced it yourself is why you will recognize it.

Lab 14 Is Your Restore Test

Section 5.3 makes a point about backups that is easy to nod at and hard to practice: a backup that has never been restored is an assumption. Lab 14 is where you actually recover data rather than reading about recovering data. Note what the process looked like and how long it took — those two details are what a manager asks about when planning a real recovery.

Screenshots Are How This Gets Graded

Each lab has its own quiz in Brightspace, and each lab's screenshots go into that lab's own quiz — not attached as separate files, and not combined into one submission. Paste each screenshot directly into its assigned question field.

Every screenshot must show the full screen, including the timestamp and your reservation ID. A cropped image that could have come from anywhere is not proof that you did the lab.

Capture the Setting Before and After

These labs change security settings rather than just reading them. Record what each setting was before you changed it and what you set it to. Unit 6 troubleshoots machines where a security setting is the cause of the symptom, and the settings you configured yourself are the ones you will recognize.
