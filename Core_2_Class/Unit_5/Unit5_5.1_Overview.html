Unit 4 taught you to recognize the threat. This unit is the answer to every one of them — the settings, protocols, and procedures that actually stop what you just learned to name.

Start With the Pre-Assessment

The Section 5.2 Pre-Assessment is an independent activity you complete on your own. Finish it by the end of Tuesday, the first day of the unit, before you begin the reading and videos, and do not study for it first. Several questions will look familiar from Unit 4, seen from the other side.

Learning Objectives

After completing this unit, you will be able to:

  1. Configure Windows security settings, including Defender Antivirus, the firewall, accounts and groups, sign-in options, and User Account Control
  2. Predict a user's effective access to a shared folder from its NTFS and share permissions, and explain how inheritance affects new files
  3. Select the appropriate encryption for a situation — BitLocker, BitLocker To Go, or the Encrypting File System
  4. Compare wireless security protocols and authentication methods, and recommend one for a given environment
  5. Apply hardening techniques to mobile devices, including encryption, screen locks, remote wipe, and managed profiles
  6. Secure a SOHO wireless and wired network by configuring router, wireless, and firewall settings
  7. Configure relevant security settings in a browser and judge the risk of extensions, certificates, and synchronization
  8. Apply change management procedures, distinguishing standard, normal, and emergency changes
  9. Justify a workstation backup and recovery plan, including backup type, rotation scheme, and how the restore is tested
Technical Overview

Unit 5 addresses CompTIA A+ Core 2 (220-1202) Domain 2.0 Security — objectives 2.2 (configure and apply basic Microsoft Windows OS security settings), 2.3 (wireless security protocols and authentication methods), 2.8 (common methods for securing mobile devices), 2.10 (security settings on SOHO wireless and wired networks), and 2.11 (relevant security settings in a browser) — and Domain 4.0 Operational Procedures, objectives 4.2 (change management procedures) and 4.3 (workstation backup and recovery methods).

In Plain Terms

Last unit you learned what can go wrong. This unit you learn what to click, type, and configure so it does not.

It covers four places a technician applies security — the Windows workstation, the wireless network, the mobile device, and the browser — plus the two procedures that protect an organization from itself: managing changes so nobody breaks production on a Friday, and keeping backups that actually restore.

Why This Unit Comes Next

Unit 4 was deliberately the recognize the threat unit. This is the configure the defense unit, and the order matters: a control you do not understand the reason for is a control you will eventually turn off for convenience.

As you read this unit, name the Unit 4 attack that each control defeats. Failed-attempt lockout answers dictionary attacks. RADIUS answers the shared wireless key that never gets changed. Backups answer ransomware. That pairing is what scenario questions are built on, and it turns two units of vocabulary into one connected picture.

Domain 2.0 Security is 28% of the exam. Units 4 and 5 together carry all of it.

Your First Full-Length Practice Exam

This unit brings your first timed, closed-book run at a complete 90-item exam. Take it in one uninterrupted sitting, with no notes and no pauses — a practice exam taken comfortably tells you nothing useful.

The number that matters is your scaled score out of 900, not a raw percentage. Core 2 passes at 700. Aim for 780 or better on two consecutive attempts — that margin is what turns a score into confidence, and we will track it from here to the end of the course.

A first attempt below 700 at this stage is normal and is information, not a verdict. You still have Unit 6, a second pass through the material, and the Final Study Exam ahead of you.

The Busiest Assessment Week

Three NETLAB labs, a unit test, and a full-length practice exam land in the same seven days. The reading is lighter to make room for exactly that. Book the labs at the start of the unit and give the practice exam a block of its own — it is worth far more taken properly on Sunday than squeezed in on Monday night.

How This Unit Is Graded

Three things are graded: the NETLAB labs (5.8), the reflection (5.9), and the unit test (5.10). Each is worth 100 points, awarded in full when it meets the completion standard on that item's page. Work that falls short is returned for revision and resubmitted for full credit — it is not partially scored.

Everything else in this unit is ungraded: the pre-assessment, your reading and video notes, the practice questions, the labs, and the practice exam. They are still expected, and they are where the learning actually happens — the graded items only measure it.

Core 2 Passes at 700, Not 675

Core 1 passed at 675 out of 900. Core 2 passes at 700. Because the scale starts at 100 rather than zero, 700 works out to about 75% of raw points — not the roughly 72% you needed in Core 1.

This is the unit where that number stops being abstract. Your first practice exam gives you a scaled score to compare it against.
