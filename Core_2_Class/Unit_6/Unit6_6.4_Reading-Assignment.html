A Note on the Book's Index

If you look up SSH in the index, you will find it printed as "SSH (SSD)" — a typo in the book. SSH is Secure Shell, the encrypted command-line access method in objective 4.9; SSD is a solid-state drive and has nothing to do with it. The page reference itself is correct.

Why a Second Pass Beats More New Reading

Material you met once and have not touched since is the material you will lose first. Spacing and retrieval — revisiting something after a gap and making yourself recall it rather than reread it — is the most reliably effective study method there is, and the gap is what makes it work.

Do the Review Questions before rereading the Exam Essentials. A question you miss tells you exactly which paragraph to go back to; rereading first just makes everything feel familiar.

Part 3 — All Ten Performance-Based Questions

Each chapter of Volume 2 ends with one. You have worked five across the term — Chapters 2, 4, 5, 6, and 9. Work the remaining five now, and redo the five you have already done.

Why All Ten, Again

Performance-based questions on the real exam often appear first and consume the most time. They are also where candidates report the most surprise. Working all ten in one sitting shows you the range of formats and builds the one habit that matters most: reading the whole scenario before touching anything.

Part 4 — The Assessment Test, Again

You took the Volume 2 Assessment Test in the front matter during Unit 1, before you knew anything. Take it again now, closed-book, and compare.

This is not a graded measure and it is not the Final Study Exam. It is the clearest picture you will get of six weeks of work — the same questions, the same you, a different set of answers.

Note-Taking for a Troubleshooting Unit

  • Build one symptom-to-cause sheet. Symptom on the left, first thing you would check on the right. That sheet is the unit.
  • Write the malware steps from memory, in order, once a day until you can do it without hesitating. Ten items, three days, and an easy mark on the exam.
  • Mark every Review Question you miss on the second pass with the unit it came from. Three misses from the same unit is where your remaining study time should go.
  • Keep a "still shaky" list. One line per topic you do not trust yet. That list, not the textbook, is what you review the night before the real exam.
What Good Notes Look Like

Reading notes are not graded. For this unit, aim for a symptom-to-cause sheet, the ten malware steps written from memory, your second-pass results marked by unit, and your "still shaky" list. Everything in this unit is due Thursday — see the Course Schedule.
