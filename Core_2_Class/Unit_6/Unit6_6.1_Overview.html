Three Days, Not Seven

Unit 6 runs Tuesday through Thursday, and every remaining deadline is Thursday. There is no weekend at the end of this one. Work Section 6.A, Exam Readiness, alongside the rest of the unit rather than saving it for the end — it is the part that affects what happens after this course finishes.

Start With the Pre-Assessment

The Section 6.2 Pre-Assessment is an independent activity you complete on your own, by the end of Tuesday. Do not study for it first. By now you know the drill, and by now you will recognize more of it than you expect — troubleshooting draws on all five previous units.

Learning Objectives

After completing this unit, you will be able to:

  1. Apply CompTIA's best-practice procedure for malware removal, in order, to an infected system
  2. Diagnose common Windows problems from their symptoms and select an appropriate resolution
  3. Troubleshoot common mobile operating system and application issues
  4. Troubleshoot mobile operating system and application security issues
  5. Troubleshoot common security issues on workstations and browsers
  6. Compare remote access technologies and select one appropriate to a situation
  7. Explain the basics of artificial intelligence and apply responsible-use principles to AI tools in IT work
  8. Judge competing diagnoses for a single set of symptoms and justify the one you would pursue first
Technical Overview

Unit 6 addresses CompTIA A+ Core 2 (220-1202) Domain 3.0 Software Troubleshooting in full — objectives 3.1 (troubleshoot common Windows OS problems), 3.2 (troubleshoot common mobile OS and application issues), 3.3 (troubleshoot common mobile OS and application security issues), and 3.4 (troubleshoot common security issues) — together with Domain 2.0 objective 2.6 (best-practice procedures for malware removal) and Domain 4.0 objectives 4.9 (remote access technologies) and 4.10 (the basics of artificial intelligence).

In Plain Terms

Everything until now has been "here is how it works" and "here is how to set it up." This unit is "here is what the user says, now what?"

That is the actual job. Nobody opens a ticket to tell you their computer is working. They tell you it is slow, or it will not start, or something strange appeared, and you have to turn that sentence into a cause.

Why This Unit Comes Last

Troubleshooting is placed at the end on purpose. You cannot recognize abnormal until you know normal, and the first five units are what normal looks like.

When a Windows machine will not boot, you are using Unit 1's installation and boot knowledge. When a profile takes four minutes to load, you reach for Unit 2's tools. When a laptop connects to Wi-Fi but reaches nothing, that is Unit 3. When a browser is redirecting, Units 4 and 5 tell you what kind of attack that is and which control failed. Nothing in this unit is new knowledge so much as new use of what you already have.

Domain 3.0 is 23% of the exam, and this unit carries all of it.

How This Unit Is Graded

Three things are graded: the NETLAB labs (6.8), the reflection (6.9), and the unit test (6.10). Each is worth 100 points, awarded in full when it meets the completion standard on that item's page. Work that falls short is returned for revision and resubmitted for full credit.

Everything else is ungraded, including the Final Study Exam. That exam is the most important ungraded thing you will do in this course — it is the number that tells you whether you are ready.

Core 2 Passes at 700, Not 675

One last time, because this is the week it stops being hypothetical. Core 1 passed at 675 out of 900. Core 2 passes at 700, which is about 75% of raw points.

Compare your Final Study Exam scaled score against that number, and against your Unit 5 practice exam. The trend between the two tells you more than either one alone.
