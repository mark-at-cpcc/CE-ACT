6.A.1 — Final Study Exam

Ninety questions, drawn from all four exam domains and all six units. Ungraded, and the most useful thing you will do this week.

Take This Properly or Do Not Take It

A practice exam taken comfortably tells you nothing. Before you start:

  • Set a timer for 90 minutes — the same length as the real exam
  • Close your notes, the textbook, and every other tab
  • Sit it in one block, with no pauses and no lookups
  • Answer every question, because there is no penalty for a wrong answer on the real exam either

The number this produces is only worth something if the conditions matched. A 790 earned with the book open is a 650 you have not found yet.

What This Exam Is

This is a full-length study exam built to the same domain weighting as the real 220-1202:

What This Exam Is Not

It is not a copy of the real exam, and nobody can give you one — see Section 6.A.0 on why obtaining real exam content ends careers. It is also not a performance-based question simulator; this format cannot reproduce those. Work all ten of the textbook's Performance-Based Questions separately, as Section 6.4 assigns.

Score by Domain, Not Just Overall

An overall 72% can hide a 50% in one domain, and the real exam will find it. When you review your results, sort your misses by domain using the table above and ask which unit each one came from.

Three misses from the same unit is not bad luck — it is where the rest of your study time should go. The honest limitation of this model is worth repeating: CompTIA does not publish its scaling, so the scaled figures here are an approximation built for this course, not a replica.

Compare It to Unit 5

You took your first full-length practice exam in Unit 5. The gap between that score and this one is more informative than either number alone. A rising trend across two timed attempts is the single best predictor you have of how exam day will go.

After You Finish

  1. Record the score and the date. You want the trend, not one number.
  2. Review every miss — and every answer you guessed correctly. A lucky guess is a gap that has not surfaced yet.
  3. Add each miss to your "still shaky" list from Section 6.4, tagged with its unit.
  4. Retake it after you have studied those topics, not immediately. The gap is what makes a retake meaningful.
  5. Use the Final Week Checklist in Section 6.A.0 to turn that list into a plan with dates on it.
