No Guarantee of Certification

This course prepares you to sit the CompTIA A+ Core 1 (220-1201) certification exam. It does not certify you, and no instructor, institution, or training provider can guarantee a passing result. The exam is administered by CompTIA and scored independently.

What determines your outcome is the work you put in over these six weeks. Students who complete the reading, watch the videos, do the labs, and hit the practice-exam threshold pass at a high rate. Students who skip the out-of-class work generally do not, regardless of how well class went.

0.2 — What This Course Covers

This course prepares you for the first half of the CompTIA A+ certification — the Core 1 exam, 220-1201. Over six weeks we cover computing hardware, mobile devices, networking fundamentals, virtualization and cloud concepts, and the troubleshooting methodology that ties all of it together.

In Plain Terms

You will learn what is inside a computer, how computers talk to each other, and how to figure out what is wrong when one stops working. It assumes you have never opened a machine in your life. That is the intended starting point, not a problem to apologize for.

Technical Overview

Instruction maps to the five domains of CompTIA A+ Core 1, exam 220-1201:

This course compresses that twelve-month profile into an accelerated six-week format, supported by structured lab work and a practice-exam mastery threshold.

675 Out of 900 Is Not 75%

This surprises nearly everyone, so learn it now. The A+ scale runs from 100 to 900, not from zero. That means the 675 cut score corresponds to roughly 72% of raw points — not 75%.

It matters because a student sitting at 70% who believes they need 75% thinks they are five points from comfortable. They are actually two points from failing. We track your scaled score all term so that number is never a surprise.

Success here comes down to a handful of consistent habits, repeated weekly:

  • Read your student email once a day
  • Read the course announcements once a day
  • Observe the class schedule and arrive on time
  • Build a personal schedule that gives you room to complete the out-of-class work

  • Use your CPCC student email to communicate with your instructor
  • Respond to emails or phone calls from the ACT team
None of This Is Complicated

It is consistency, not brilliance. Students who show up, check their email daily, and ask questions early almost always do well in this course. You've got this.

How Your Work Is Graded

Every assignment in this course is worth 100 points and is awarded in full when it meets the completion standard published on that assignment's page. Work that falls short is returned with feedback and resubmitted for full credit — it is not partially scored.

That structure exists so you can stop calculating your grade and focus on the only number that decides anything: your scaled score on full-length practice exams. Before I recommend you schedule the real exam, you need two consecutive practice exams at 780 scaled or better. That is deliberately well above the 675 cut, leaving margin for test-day nerves and unfamiliar phrasing.

0.10 — Using NETLAB

Part of your hands-on work in this course happens in NETLAB+, a remote lab platform you reach through your browser. It is where you will complete the NDG A+ v5 lab series — real systems, not simulations.

In Plain Terms

NETLAB hands you a real computer over the internet for a scheduled block of time. You book a slot, do the lab, and when your time is up the system wipes the machine clean for the next student. You cannot permanently break anything, which is the whole point.

Technical Overview

NETLAB+ provisions pods of physical and virtual equipment to reservation-based student sessions. Each pod is allocated exclusively for the duration of the reservation and reset to a clean baseline on release. Because pods are finite and allocated per course, availability is genuinely constrained near deadlines.

Getting In

  1. Always launch from Brightspace. Each lab has its own link inside the unit that assigns it. Do not navigate to the NETLAB site directly — launching from the course shell is what associates your session with this class and your roster entry.
  2. Make a reservation. Choose a date and time window. NETLAB allocates a pod to you for exactly that period.
  3. Read the worksheet before your window opens. Worksheets live in the NETLAB — Lab Worksheets folder under Coursework in Brightspace, and inside NETLAB itself. Reading during your reservation burns time you paid for.
  4. Complete the lab in one continuous session, capturing screenshots as you go.
  5. Submit screenshots to the matching lab quiz in Brightspace.
One Session, No Save Point

If your reservation expires mid-lab, the environment resets and you begin again from step one. There is no pause, no saved progress, and no partial completion.

Book generously. If a lab is estimated at 1.25 hours, reserve two. Finishing early costs you nothing; running out costs you the entire attempt.

Pods Are Limited — Reserve Early

This course has a finite number of lab pods. When the whole class books the same Sunday night, the slots are simply gone, and the deadline does not move because a reservation was unavailable.

Book your window early in the week. This is the most common avoidable way students lose points.

"But It's Free" Is Not a Defense

This is the misunderstanding that gets people in trouble, so it is worth stating plainly. A copyright owner who chooses to distribute a work at no cost gives up none of their exclusive rights. Zero price and zero restrictions are unrelated concepts.

Downloading a free video and re-uploading it elsewhere is precisely as infringing as doing the same to a paid one. The absence of a paywall is a business decision, not a license.

"It's for Education" Is Also Not a Blanket Defense

Fair use is a fact-specific legal doctrine assessed case by case under four statutory factors, and educational purpose is only one consideration within one of those factors. It is not a checkbox that authorizes copying, and it does not permit wholesale reproduction and redistribution of an entire commercially valuable work.

Nobody in this course — including your instructor — is in a position to tell you a particular use qualifies as fair use. Do not treat "educational" as permission.
